1 · Intro

When to use it.

Payroll costing decides which costing string (COA or POET) pays for a person's compensation, and for which periods. Each payroll run turns pay into costing results that move to the General Ledger, so wrong costing means wrong department, grant, and project financials.

An assignment with no costing is not an error: the work unit default applies. Changes to a job or funding may require a costing update, and fixing costing after payroll runs takes a retro cost transfer.

Use this whenYou set up or review costing for a hire, transfer, or funding change, or you need to decide whether an employee can stay on the default.

Watch-outs

  • Costing affects budgets, and some costing changes can't be corrected. Validate with your FUM.
  • Costing touches payroll and financial reporting. Wrong costing charges the wrong budget.
  • Open item: explain how a department sets default costing at the work unit or business unit level.
  • Open item: confirm whether default costing shows on the costing reports or appears blank. Some schools cost everyone.
  • Open item: replace the program code example with one most areas recognize, and link the costing by person information.
  • After a business unit transfer, costing does not reset on its own. Review it every time.
  • Blank costing on an assignment is not wrong by itself; the work unit default applies. Confirm the default is where the pay should go.
  • Open item: confirm the criteria for retro cost transfers and the deadline to fix costing before a payroll run. This covers more than grant labor.
  • Costing follows an employee who changes business units. The HCM receiving the transfer reviews the COA and POET costing so payroll is charged to the new funding source.
  • Most of these scenarios don't have walkthroughs recorded yet. Ask your FUM when unsure.
  • Salary distributed across multiple funding sources and cross-unit award compensation may become reference resources rather than core training. To be decided by the HCM group.

Costing essentials: what it is, why it matters, and when to update it

Use when: You set up or review costing for a hire, transfer, or funding change, or you need to decide whether an employee can stay on the default. · System: Oracle HCM

  1. Know the three levels Vanderbilt uses.
    • Element (a type of pay within the assignment, such as a lump sum): if costed, that pay is charged to the element's costing string. This is how you charge a lump sum to a different department.
    • Assignment (the person's job): if costed, all pay on the job follows it, except elements costed on their own.
    • Work unit default: with no specific costing, pay goes to the work unit's entity and financial unit, general unrestricted funds (05), the natural account for that type of pay, and program and activity 000.
  2. Open Costing for Persons.
  3. Search the person with Advanced Search and the right Effective As-of Date.
    • Use today's date (or the first day of the current pay period) to see what applies now.
  4. Select the person, then select an element or assignment on the left.
  5. Read what you see.
  6. Ask your FUM whether your area uses program and activity codes.
  7. Ask your FUM or grants manager whether effort must be costed to a POET string.
  8. Click Done.
  9. Costing is set in Costing for Persons.
  10. Oracle uses the most specific level first: element, then assignment, then work unit default.
    • Element > Assignment > Work unit default
  11. Payroll creates costing results, then transfers them to the General Ledger.
  12. Costing must be right before payroll runs.
  13. Costing follows a person who transfers business units (BUs).
  14. After payroll runs, a retro cost transfer (RCT) is needed.
  15. Check your area with the Departmental Playbook reports.
  16. How to check costing for any event below.
  17. New hire: is the costing right for every funding source?
  18. A change within the work unit: is the funding still correct?
  19. Transfer to another work unit or business unit: the new area updates costing.
  20. Bonus, lump sum, or interim pay: should it charge a different funding source than salary?
  21. Another business unit funds an award for your employee.
  22. Salary distributed across multiple funding sources.
  23. Before every HCM Transaction Cutoff: check costing for your unit.
